How to read this

Two things make a steering linkage "connect well": the wheels turn symmetrically with the right amount of Ackermann, and they don't steer themselves as the suspension moves. This tool covers both, one per tab.

Steering & Ackermann

The single degree of freedom is the lateral travel of the two inner pickups — whether that's a rack sliding sideways or the output balls of a servo-saver bellcrank. Everything downstream (track rods → steering arms → wheels) follows from it.

  • Arm angle sets Ackermann. Angle the steering arms so their centre-lines, extended rearward, meet near the rear axle — that's ~100% Ackermann (inner wheel turns more than outer, so it scrubs less in a turn). The panel shows the ideal angle for your track and wheelbase.
  • Match the throw. At centre, keep the track rod roughly perpendicular to the steering arm. If left and right locks differ a lot, or a link folds past straight, the plan view and the warning chips will flag it.
  • Servo sizing is geometry, not guesswork. The horn only has to produce enough symmetric lateral travel at the pickups. Horn travel ≈ horn length × sin(throw); the sizing box back-solves the horn length you need.

Bump steer

As the wheel moves up and down, the track-rod outer ball swings on an arc about its inner pivot. If that arc doesn't match the arc the upright wants to follow, the wheel toes in or out on its own — bump steer. It makes a car dartey over bumps and under braking.

  • Aim the tie rod at the instant centre. In front view, the upper and lower arm lines meet at the suspension's instant centre. The track rod should point at it, with its length tuned so the outer ball's arc tracks the upright. The tool draws the instant centre and reports the aim error.
  • Keep it flat around ride height. A little bump steer at full droop is normal; what matters is the slope through the middle of the travel — read it off the curve and the "rate @ here" figure.

Recommended architecture for an IFS 4WD front

Servo (mounted to the chassis) → short drag link → servo-saver bellcrank on a centre post → two track rods out to the steering arms. The bellcrank does the height transfer and the one-to-two split, so no single link runs at a bad angle. Always keep a servo saver in the load path.
